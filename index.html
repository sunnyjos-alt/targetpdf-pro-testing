Frequently asked questions

Everything you might want to know before uploading.

Is TargetPDF really free?

Yes — completely. No login, no usage limits, no watermark, no hidden charges, no premium tier. Compression runs on your device, so we have no server costs to pass on.

Do you upload my PDF to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser using WebAssembly. Your PDF is never transmitted anywhere. You can verify this by opening DevTools → Network tab while compressing — you'll see no upload request.

What does "exact target size" mean?

Instead of choosing "low / medium / high" compression and hoping, you type the number you need — 500 KB, 1 MB, 200 KB — and TargetPDF finds the best quality that fits under that number. It's the difference between guessing and knowing.

Will my text become blurry?

No. Text is never touched — only images get resized to hit your target. If the PDF is scanned (image-only), we detect that and warn you before compressing.

Why does it sometimes say "cannot compress below X KB"?

Every PDF has a floor — the smallest size it can reach without becoming unreadable. When your target is below that floor, we tell you the honest minimum and offer to compress to it instead. We don't silently hand you a blurry file.

How long does compression take?

Small PDFs (under 1 MB) finish in 2–5 seconds. Large scanned documents can take 30–60 seconds because all the work happens on your device. You'll see a progress bar the whole time.

What's the maximum file size?

10 MB. Larger files would take too long to process locally. For very big documents, a dedicated desktop tool like Ghostscript is still faster.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. The tool is fully responsive. On mobile, PDF previews open in a new tab instead of side-by-side, since phone browsers don't render PDFs inline.

Will my signatures and stamps survive compression?

Yes. Signatures and stamps are part of the page image or vector layer, and Ghostscript preserves them by default. We only resize embedded images to reduce size.

Can I compress multiple PDFs at once?

Not yet. Batch compression is on our roadmap. For now, compress one file, download it, then compress the next.
